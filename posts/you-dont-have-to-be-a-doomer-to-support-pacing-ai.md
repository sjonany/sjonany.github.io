# You don't have to be a doomer to support pacing AI progress
*Published 2026-11-25*

*Tags: [#computing](/tags/computing.html), [#ai-safety](/tags/ai-safety.html)*

This month, I [started off](https://sjonany.github.io/posts/ai-safety-dinner-conversations.html) with the intuition that we should balance AI safety effort with AI capability growth. However, I didn't feel comfortable coming up with my own [p(doom)](https://youtu.be/9U-FdOWTxPw?si=C3BRzzHtajES6I0b&t=547). After a lot more reading, I **still** don't feel comfortable making up my own number.

**Why aren't you a doomer yet?** A part of it is almost certainly the [absurdity bias](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/absurdity-bias-neom-edition). Saying "AI existential risk" makes me feel like I'm wearing a tinfoil hat. Another part is that for such a strong claim I would want to do a lot of careful studying, but I don't think doing a third pass on [METR reports](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/) and all the [Substack commentaries](https://blog.karthiktadepalli.com/p/asi-2027) is necessarily the best use of my time.

**Directional alignment as a license to start.** So! The process of writing this post, and extracting a snippet of the most influential readings, is me officially giving myself a license to say that it is **enough** that I am [directionally aligned](https://substack.com/home/post/p-225956895) that we should pace AI progress. "How far am I on the doomer scale" is no longer a productive inquiry at the moment, and I would rather move on to figuring out the **concrete action items** I can take.

Okay, now here is a brief packet on "what and why pace AI progress even if you're not a doomer."

## What's pacing?
I'll just quote [Dario](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) (emphasis mine):
 >We must **slow the pace** at which we improve the **capabilities** of AI models. ... building AI at a balanced rate that aims to **ensure its safety** while still achieving its benefits and grappling with important geopolitical dilemmas.

**My take.** The **current balance** of AI capability and AI safety measures is out of whack, as shown by [real incidents](https://huggingface.wtf/) of AI agents going rogue. The **rate of progress** in AI capability is also alarming, as we [have already seen](https://youtu.be/1NHPDzpfR6Y?si=oQxtgBNP8gGxLd4b&t=134) in math. If we do nothing, the gap will only widen. We need to correct this imbalance.

## Why pace AI progress?
**Doomer side.** See [AI 2027's answer](https://pacing-ai.com/#why-pace). Even if you don't buy the RSI argument, as long as you agree that "AI today vs 2 years ago is wildly different", the risks mentioned still apply.

**Non-doomer view 1: AI as normal tech.** This [blog post](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/the-ai-as-normal-technology-view) is from the camp that claims that AI should be treated as [another normal technology](https://youtu.be/PoDop1H64-s?si=PeLRIlT72iUXoNVI&t=94). They too agreed that we should invest a lot more work in AI safety. Here are some quotes.

Doubt on cybersecurity defense being ready (emphasis mine)
>Combined with the fact that deployment barriers for harmful uses are often lower than for economically productive uses, risks such as in cybersecurity can arise rapidly, leading to periods where the offense-defense balance is upset since **defenders might not have time to adapt**. Thus, we are no longer confident in our earlier prediction that the offense-defense balance will continue to hold even with advances in AI.

Need more safety now (emphasis mine)
>while we continue to reject the alarmism, and don’t believe that catastrophic risks are imminent, we do think risks are increasing over time because our investments and policies aren’t keeping pace. And while the specific types of investments and policies we call for overlap only partly with that of the safety community, we agree that we are **not currently on track**.

**Non-doomer view 2: Steven Pinker.** This [essay](https://quillette.com/2026/11/23/will-we-all-go-together-when-we-go-ai-alignment-scott-alexander-doomerism/) has a similar flavor. There is an agreement on danger and the need for safety measures, but not necessarily on extinction.
>Alexander and I don’t disagree about whether AI can be dangerous, nor whether we should work to make it safer. ... The hazards are obvious enough. AI can supercharge cyberattacks, fraud, and disinformation. It could lower the barriers to making biological weapons. Autonomous agents turned loose without supervision can wreak havoc, as the Hugging Face incident of this past summer showed. And the technology will bring wrenching changes to many professions. Any of these could cause harm, possibly a disaster—though falling well short of exterminating the species.

## What next?
I think that's enough effort spent on situating myself in the "doomer vs non-doomer scale". Let's start taking actions. Here are some directions I want to dive deeper into.

**How to pace.** [Dario's "how to pace" section](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier#pacing-within-democracies) has a list of strategies at the geopolitical scale, and [80,000 Hours](https://substack.com/home/post/p-226436170) offers another list that's tailored for individual actors. Go through them and decide which actions I want to take.

**Technical AI safety.** Figure out what to do with the time pacing buys to improve AI safety measures. Given my background in software engineering, I'm planning to go through [Google DeepMind's "An Approach to Technical AGI Safety and Security"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01849) and the [2026 Hinton lectures](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Ef0ocEoBCs&list=PLEX6LQ9dGFuA). If you have other recommendations, please send them my way!
